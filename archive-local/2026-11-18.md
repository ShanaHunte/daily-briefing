### Cost of living watch: utilities, benefits, financial aid

As a North Carolina public policy and cost-of-living analyst, I have synthesized the major developments from the last 48 hours. The most significant news impact for Triangle residents is the North Carolina Utilities Commission’s (NCUC) decision on Duke Energy’s multi-year rate plan and new protections against data center energy costs.

</section>

<section class="card" markdown="1">

### 1. NC Electric Rates, Duke Energy & Utility Bill Relief
The NCUC has officially approved a rate hike for Duke Energy customers, effective **January 1, 2027** (following the current 2026 cycle). While Duke Energy initially sought higher returns, the commission moderated the impact on residential households while implementing a first-of-its-kind "Data Center Tariff."

*   **The Update:** Residential customers will see an average increase of approximately **9%** over the next two years. However, new rules require large data centers to pay "contribution-to-construction" costs upfront, ensuring residential payers don't subsidize the massive infrastructure needed for Big Tech. [Source](https://www.wral.com/story/nc-approves-duke-energy-rate-hikes-new-rules-for-data-centers/21723784/) | [Source](https://www.newsobserver.com/news/business/article295679549.html)
*   **Actionable Savings:**
    *   **LIEAP (Low Income Energy Assistance Program):** Applications typically open **December 1** for households with a person aged 60+ or those receiving DAAS services. General enrollment begins **January 1**.
    *   **Duke Energy "Usage Alerts":** With rates rising in the new year, residents should enroll in [Usage Alerts](https://www.duke-energy.com/home/billing/usage-alerts) to receive mid-cycle notifications if their bill is trending higher than usual.
    *   **Lifeline Program:** If you receive Medicaid or SNAP, you qualify for $9.25 off your phone or internet bill. [Check Eligibility](https://www.lifelinesupport.org/).

</section>

<section class="card" markdown="1">

### 2. Unincorporated Wake County: Well Water & Property Taxes
While the headlines focused on utility rates, unincorporated residents in areas like Willow Spring, New Hill, and North Raleigh should prepare for year-end administrative deadlines.

*   **PFAS/Groundwater Watch:** The NCUC’s recent Duke Energy ruling also touched on coal ash remediation costs—costs that often influence groundwater safety discussions. Unincorporated residents on private wells should utilize Wake County’s **subsidized well testing kits** ($73–$150) to check for "forever chemicals" (PFAS). [Source](https://www.wake.gov/departments-government/water-quality-and-wells/well-water-testing)
*   **Property Tax Relief:** The deadline for the **NC Property Tax Exclusion** (for seniors/disabled veterans) has passed for 2026, but if you have had a significant change in health or income, you should contact the Wake County Tax Office now to prepare for the **January 1, 2027** filing window for the "Circuit Breaker" Tax Deferment Program.

</section>

<section class="card" markdown="1">

### 3. Raleigh Fuel Prices, NC Gas Tax & Commuting
Fuel volatility continues to impact Triangle commuters. Analysts are monitoring the NC Gas Tax, which is subject to annual adjustments based on population and energy inflation.

*   **Gas Tax Update:** The current NC motor fuel tax sits at **40.4 cents per gallon**. As of November 18, 2026, Raleigh fuel prices are averaging lower than the national trend, but the Duke Energy rate hike for EV charging infrastructure may affect those transitioning to electric vehicles. [Source](https://www.carolinajournal.com/nc-regulators-ok-duke-energy-rate-hikes-starting-jan-1/)
*   **Commuter Savings:** GoTriangle is currently evaluating route efficiencies for 2027. Residents can use the **GoPerks program** to earn rewards for carpooling or using transit, offsetting the rising costs of private vehicle maintenance.

</section>

<section class="card" markdown="1">

### 4. NC Medicaid (Healthy Blue) & Healthcare Benefits
North Carolina continues its "unwinding" process and expansion of Medicaid services. Policy updates this week emphasize the importance of beneficiary contact information.

*   **The Update:** If you are a **Healthy Blue (Blue Cross NC)** member, you must ensure your current mailing address is on file with the ePASS portal. Thousands of North Carolinians risk losing coverage due to "procedural denials" (mail sent to the wrong address). [Source](https://epass.nc.gov/)
*   **Expansion Opportunity:** If your household income is below 138% of the Federal Poverty Level (approx. $20,000 for a single adult), you are eligible for NC Medicaid. There is no "open enrollment" period for Medicaid; you can apply year-round.

</section>

<section class="card" markdown="1">

### 5. College Financial Aid & Wake County Youth Programs
With new reports highlighting that standard financial aid often falls short of the total cost of attendance, Raleigh families must look toward "professional judgment" appeals.

*   **FAFSA Income-Loss Appeals:** If your 2026 income was lower than your 2024 tax returns (the data used for current FAFSA filings), you can file a **Special Circumstances Appeal**. This is critical for families affected by recent tech layoffs in the Research Triangle Park (RTP). [Source](https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/2026/11/16/how-to-pay-for-college-financial-aid/76113888007/)
*   **Wake County Youth Action:** 
    *   **Wake County 4-H:** Enrollment for 2027 youth leadership programs in Raleigh and Wendell begins shortly. These programs offer low-cost alternatives to private extracurriculars.
    *   **NC 529 Plan:** North Carolina residents can contribute to the NC 529 plan with as little as $25. Contributions grow tax-free for K-12 tuition or college expenses. [Source](https://www.cfnc.org/save-for-college/)

</section>
