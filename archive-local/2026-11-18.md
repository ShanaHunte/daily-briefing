### 1. NC Electric Rates, Duke Energy & Utility Bill Relief
The North Carolina Utilities Commission (NCUC) has officially approved a significant two-year rate increase for Duke Energy customers, set to impact household budgets immediately in the new year.

*   **Rate Increase Effective Jan. 1:** Regulators approved a rate hike of approximately **9%** for residential customers. This is part of a broader plan to fund grid reliability and the transition to cleaner energy. [Source](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMib0FVX3lxTE02Wk81X1hyTTJNWXhBSXFhUktWZWNTMHVneDZLVEM5NlBvTEZiZ2F1NHoyQk4wd05VcWdGeG1BYWp5SlBSMEhfV1JVeDVSTkRlS0EzVzByRlZqS0ZQOFNpb29RSjU2TnBDYVZqR2RFQdIBb0FVX3lxTE5VWDF3ZkpHQTdJblhqZ0Izc19EOGhTSEd5ckdpRHgzOXFSdkNjVkJ4WUxGOFlFMkloSGZ0VDdvX2ktQTJlU2xva2NtLWJ5MXlwOWp1a1Z3XzhRbF9xNnVlanJpRFZoMEtuZUwxbHM1SQ?oc=5)
*   **Data Center Protections:** In a win for residential advocates, the NCUC established new safeguards requiring high-demand data centers to bear a larger share of infrastructure costs, preventing residential households from disproportionately subsidizing these large-scale users. [Source](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiuAFBVV95cUxOdjZ3cUkyUUJFSDFPQkNBRjNNdnMyREJ2TmFqc1c1R0FpSVRJUWY1cC1reW1RTWIzZ3dmQVJTdjVGQ2F4VnkybmgybkdnTWlLeDFoZEJoeHY5RktzOHhZVndLaVJwQ1VpQU45VU9TTHRnVmFuMERFUjctNnlBSWg1bWU2Sm1QdGdZbGZtQmg5QUNlX0xZbG5aMEZPMVZPTjdRUEtEaERuWTd2N2drTzVqelF3Um5QZ01R?oc=5)
*   **Savings Opportunity (LIEAP):** With bills rising Jan. 1, residents should prepare for the **Low Income Energy Assistance Program (LIEAP)** application window. Historically, NC begins taking applications for seniors and people with disabilities on **December 1**, opening to all other eligible households on **January 1**.
*   **Phone Bill Relief:** If your household income is at or below 135% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines, apply for the **Lifeline** program to receive a monthly discount on phone or broadband service. [Source](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi5AFBVV95cUxNUjI0ck1ad3pDcmY1dS12OU45MEgzZGlUUjllZms0RWpzZ0xfMWpXS21QOFdYTldUM0J6X2hneU91UE5nRW5LOVc0M25oaW9XRll0UUp5a3dlMFVnTUtQbDRIUFcwUkw1LTNNbVRfRl9OUVN6aUhidXhvbEZtcFhKRENlaUxVTDV2VkZGN3NxeldvQm1SQ05ucmJuakFGWjZLaXlFUzNEaTFDTmc3YTdUcW42OUE4bi1kWTM1eDVzbXV5UUxOY1c3QThPUC1uc0E5T2tVR0lnQlR6NkkySVRLNjBadzM?oc=5)

### 2. Unincorporated Wake County: Well Water, Trash & Taxes
While headlines focus on the energy grid, homeowners in unincorporated areas of the county face specific infrastructure shifts.

*   **PFAS and Well Water Safety:** Following the state’s focus on grid "modernization," Wake County environmental officials continue to urge private well owners to test for PFAS ("forever chemicals").
*   **Property Tax Relief:** Wake County’s "Tax Relief for Seniors and Disabled" (Circuit Breaker) applications typically have a deadline in the spring. If your utility bills are rising due to the new Duke Energy rates, ensuring your tax exemptions are updated is a critical offset.
*   **Action Step:** Residents in unincorporated areas should audit their trash collection contracts; as Raleigh expands, several private haulers in the Triangle have adjusted fuel surcharges in response to state-level energy shifts.

</section>

### 4. NC Medicaid (Healthy Blue) & State Healthcare Updates
In tandem with rising living costs, keeping healthcare coverage active is essential for Raleigh families.

*   **Redetermination Readiness:** NC Medicaid (including Healthy Blue and other plans) continues its "unwinding" process. Ensure your address is current in the **ePASS portal**.
*   **Impact of Utility Hikes:** For households on the margin of Medicaid eligibility, the increase in mandatory utility costs starting Jan. 1 emphasizes the importance of utilizing the **CIP (Crisis Intervention Program)** if a medical emergency creates a choice between paying for heat or healthcare.

</section>

With the cost of attendance rising at NC universities, families must look beyond the initial FAFSA offer.

*   **FAFSA Income-Loss Appeals:** If your household income has decreased since the tax year used for your FAFSA (e.g., due to job loss or medical expenses), you can file a **Professional Judgment Appeal** with the financial aid office at schools like NC State or Wake Tech. [Source](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMipwFBVV95cUxQQy1vcTVJNjhiNjJCdUJtWm51U0t4TmxsNF93aUZyc2hlVXJaY3YtSk9BUFNVVlJfcTZyaGlBSExqbWFISkVjdXpOdEtaS203SmF0VTZRclJvM2RIRWYyaE9lWmt0QjRwWFRWQTg1S2t4SmhDemVwQXFQZThEanl3YnRFeXlnRnV4dmRsZHd0eHFUYmpTeU5nT0xlQzJYdU1JZzRVc2hXMA?oc=5)
